2. Executive Summary

The question. About 4 in 10 bookings in this dataset were cancelled. I looked at where those cancellations concentrate and how room rates (ADR) behave, so a hotel could target its effort instead of treating every booking the same.

The data. 119,390 bookings and 32 columns from a City Hotel and a Resort Hotel, with arrivals from 2015 to 2017.

The approach. I cleaned the data (date conversion, one extreme ADR record removed), then explored cancellations by hotel type, month, country and market segment, and tracked ADR over time, using Python, Pandas, Matplotlib and Seaborn. The analysis is exploratory and descriptive.

What stands out

  • 37.04% of all bookings were cancelled.
  • The City Hotel cancels at 41.73%. The Resort Hotel is visibly lower (≈28%).
  • Resort ADR is very seasonal (≈40–60 in winter, above 200 in summer peaks). City Hotel ADR is steadier.
  • January is the only month where cancellations outnumber completed bookings (≈6,050 vs ≈4,600).
  • Portugal (PRT) makes up 70.07% of cancellations among the top 10 countries.
  • Group bookings are 16.6% of bookings but 27.4% of cancellations. Direct bookings are 10.6% of bookings but 4.4% of cancellations.
  • Cancelled bookings tend to sit at higher ADR than completed ones from 2016 onward (visual pattern only, not tested).

So what. Cancellation risk is uneven. It is concentrated in the City Hotel, in group bookings, in certain months and in one main source country, which gives a clear starting point for testing retention measures.

3. Business Problem

Around 4 in 10 bookings in this data end in a cancellation (37.04%). For a hotel, a cancelled booking is a room that has to be sold again, which makes revenue and room planning harder.

Decisions this analysis is meant to support

Where to focus cancellation-reduction effort Cancellations by hotel type, month, country and market segment (9.2, 9.4, 9.6, 9.7)
How to plan pricing across the year ADR over time at each hotel (9.3)
Which bookings may carry more risk ADR of cancelled vs completed bookings (9.8)

4. Project Objectives

  1. Measure the overall cancellation rate.
  2. Compare cancellations between the City Hotel and the Resort Hotel.
  3. Understand how ADR changes over time for each hotel.
  4. Find out when (which months) and where (which countries and market segments) cancellations concentrate.
  5. Check whether cancelled bookings differ from completed ones in ADR.

Scope: exploratory and descriptive. No predictive modelling or statistical testing.

5. Research Questions

  1. What share of bookings gets cancelled?
  2. Do the City Hotel and the Resort Hotel cancel at different rates?
  3. How does ADR move over time at each hotel, and is there seasonality?
  4. Do cancellations vary by month?
  5. Which countries account for the most cancellations?
  6. Which market segments account for more (or fewer) cancellations than their share of bookings?
  7. Do cancelled bookings have a different ADR from completed bookings?

9. Exploratory Data Analysis

Each block follows the same pattern: business question, analysis, visualization, finding, business interpretation. The tag under each heading says whether the finding was calculated (printed in the notebook) or read visually from a chart.

9.1 Just over a third of bookings are cancelled

Descriptive · calculated proportion

Business question: How big is the cancellation problem overall?

Analysis: Share of is_canceled values across all bookings.

Visualization: A simple bar chart, the clearest way to compare two counts.

Finding: 37.04% of bookings were cancelled (about 44,200) and 62.96% were not (about 75,200).

Business interpretation: Roughly 1 in every 3 bookings doesn't turn into a stay. That is the baseline everything else is compared against.

9.2 The City Hotel cancels more often than the Resort Hotel

Descriptive · City rate calculated, Resort rate read from chart

Business question: Is the cancellation problem the same at both hotels?

Analysis: Cancelled vs not-cancelled counts per hotel, plus the cancellation share for each.

Visualization: Grouped bar chart (countplot), which shows counts and the cancelled/not split side by side.

Finding: City Hotel cancellation rate is 41.73% (58.27% not cancelled). The notebook computed the Resort Hotel share but didn't print it. The bars show ≈11,000 cancelled vs ≈29,000 not, about ≈28%. City Hotel also has about twice as many bookings (79,330 vs 40,060).

Business interpretation: Most cancelled bookings come from the City Hotel, so it is the natural place to start.

9.3 Resort pricing follows the seasons, City pricing is steadier

Visual · line chart, values read from chart

Business question: Do the two hotels price the same way through the year?

Analysis: Mean ADR per reservation status date, separately for each hotel.

Visualization: A two-line time series, best for trends and seasons.

Finding: Resort ADR swings hard with the seasons. It drops to about ≈40–60 in winter (late 2015 to early 2016, and late 2016 to early 2017) and climbs to about ≈210–225 in summer (August 2016, August–September 2017). City ADR stays in a narrower band (roughly ≈80–150) and creeps upward across the period. In summer the Resort is priced above the City Hotel, and in winter well below. The early-2015 lines are jumpy because there are very few records then, so I don't read much into them.

Business interpretation: Resort income depends heavily on peak season, while the City Hotel earns more evenly. Pricing and promotions probably need different approaches.

9.4 January is the toughest month for cancellations

Visual · bar chart, values read from chart

Business question: Are some months worse for cancellations?

Analysis: Count of cancelled vs not-cancelled bookings by month (taken from reservation_status_date).

Visualization: Grouped bar chart by month.

Finding: January is the only month where cancellations (≈6,050) outnumber completed bookings (≈4,600). December is close (≈3,520 cancelled vs ≈3,870 completed). July has a spike of ≈4,370 cancellations. August is the strongest month for completed bookings (≈8,550) with only ≈2,700 cancelled.

Business interpretation: Cancellations are seasonal. Note that "month" here is the month the status was recorded, not the arrival month.

9.5 January and July carry the most cancelled ADR

Descriptive · calculated sums

Business question: In which months do cancelled bookings carry the most rate value?

Analysis: Sum of ADR for cancelled bookings, grouped by month.

Visualization: Bar chart by month.

Finding: January is highest at 571,261, then July at 482,431 and February at 447,916. September is lowest at 275,528, so January is just over 2× September.

Business interpretation: The pattern matches the count chart above: January and July are the heaviest months. One caution: this sum grows with the number of cancellations and isn't real revenue (ADR is a nightly rate and stay length isn't included). It is a pricing-weight indicator only.

9.6 Portugal leads cancellations, and also leads bookings

Descriptive · shares within the top 10 only

Business question: Where do cancelled guests come from?

Analysis: Top 10 countries by number of cancelled bookings.

Visualization: Pie chart of shares within the top 10.

Finding: PRT is 70.07%, followed by GBR 6.25%, ESP 5.54%, FRA 4.92%, ITA 3.39%, DEU 3.10%, IRL 2.12%, BRA 2.11%, USA 1.28% and BEL 1.21%. These shares are out of the top 10 only, not out of all cancellations.

Business interpretation: One country dominates. But PRT is also the biggest source of bookings overall (48,590 of 119,390, about 40.7%), so this chart alone can't say whether Portuguese guests cancel at a higher rate. That comparison wasn't calculated.

9.7 Groups cancel above their share, Direct and Corporate below

Descriptive · calculated proportions

Business question: Do some booking sources cancel more than their share of bookings?

Analysis: Each segment's share of all bookings compared with its share of cancelled bookings.

Visualization: Two proportion tables (no chart in the notebook).

9.8 Cancelled bookings tend to be priced higher (a lead, not a result)

Visual only · not tested

Business question: Are higher-priced bookings more likely to be cancelled?

Analysis: Mean ADR per date, split into cancelled and not-cancelled bookings.

Visualization: Two-line time series to compare the two groups across the same dates.

Finding: From early 2016 the cancelled line sits above the not-cancelled line most of the time. The gap looks widest around January–May 2017 (roughly ≈100–150 cancelled vs ≈70–110 not). The two lines almost meet in autumn 2016. Both rise into summer. Before mid-2015, the cancelled line is very volatile and the not-cancelled line hasn't started yet, so I don't read much into that part.

Business interpretation: It hints that pricier bookings may be cancelled more often. This is only a visual comparison with no test, and it doesn't separate hotel type or season, so I treat it as a lead worth checking, not a result.

12. Conclusion & Project Resources

What the analysis found. All seven research questions are answered descriptively. Just over a third of bookings (37.04%) are cancelled, and the risk is uneven: higher at the City Hotel, higher for group bookings, concentrated in January and July, and led by one country (PRT). Resort pricing follows the seasons far more than City pricing, and cancelled bookings seem to be priced higher than completed ones.

What the business can take from it. Cancellation effort can be aimed at specific hotels, channels and months rather than spread across everything.

What it can't say yet. This is exploratory. It shows where the patterns are, not why they happen.

Sensible next steps

  • Calculate cancellation rates by country and market segment, not just shares.
  • Explore variables not yet analysed (lead time, deposit type, customer type, previous cancellations).
  • Test key differences statistically before acting on them.

13. Limitations

About the data

  • Old data. Arrivals are from 2015–2017, so patterns may have changed.
  • Source and representativeness. The dataset source isn't stated. About 66% of records are City Hotel and about 41% of bookings are from PRT, so results may not generalise.
  • Data quality. A negative ADR (−6.38) wasn't addressed, missing children and country values were left as is, and a duplicate check is not available. The 5,000 outlier cutoff was a judgement call from the box plot.

About the method

  • Descriptive only. No statistical tests or models were used, so nothing here shows cause and effect.
  • Date used for monthly and time charts. They use reservation_status_date, not the arrival date, so "month" means when the status was recorded.
  • Shares, not rates. The country pie and segment tables compare shares. Cancellation rates by country and segment weren't calculated.
  • ADR totals aren't revenue. Summed ADR is a rough indicator only.

About scope

  • Variables not analysed. Lead time, deposit type, customer type, previous cancellations and special requests were not explored, so drivers of cancellation can't be concluded.
